SLIDE 6 — Key Machine Components
=================================

Let me now describe the individual physical components of the machine.

Number one is the machining enclosure — a fully sealed workspace with special interlocked protective doors. The laser safety class is Class 1, meaning no laser radiation escapes when the doors are closed.

Number two is the scanner head, mounted above the table. It consists of a system of lenses and mirrors that deflect the laser beam dynamically within the focal plane, covering a range of plus or minus 60 mm without any axis movement.

Number three is the rotary-tilt table with B and C axes, equipped with EROWA clamping. Since laser machining is a non-contact process — no cutting forces are generated — workpieces can even be fixed with simple adhesive tape, as documented in experiments at our faculty.

Numbers four and five are the machine control unit and the standalone control PC. These are two separate systems: the control unit handles the CNC axis movements, while the PC handles the scanner and laser source through LaserSoft 3D software.

Number six is the CCD camera with up to 50 times magnification, adjustable in X and Y. It was used in experiments to capture images of the machined surface directly in the machine.

Number seven is the 3D measuring probe — visible in the photo on the right. It allows fast workpiece alignment and can measure the actual material removal depth without removing the part from the machine.

Finally, numbers eight and nine are the external extraction unit, which removes ablation particles and metallic vapour, and the water cooling unit for the torque discs and laser source.
