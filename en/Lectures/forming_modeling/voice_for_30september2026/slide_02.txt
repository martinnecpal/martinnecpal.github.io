Motivation: from press-shop trials to virtual try-out

Let me start with a question. Imagine you have to bring a new forging into production. How many tool trials would you expect before the first good part comes out of the press?
Without simulation, the honest answer from practice is "several". Look at the upper loop in the diagram: you design the tool, manufacture it, try it on the press, find a defect – a fold, an underfill, a crack – and go back to modify the tool. Each such loop costs weeks and a lot of money. Forging dies and stamping tools easily cost tens of thousands of euros.
There is a second argument too. Most of the cost of a product is determined already at the design stage, long before production starts. Decisions made early are cheap to change on paper and very expensive to change in steel.
Simulation moves this loop into the computer – that is the red loop at the bottom. You change the preform, the die radius or the lubricant and run the calculation again. That takes hours, not weeks.
The result is fewer tool modifications, faster production ramp-up and less material lost in flash and scrap. But note the sentence at the bottom: validation on the press is still necessary. Simulation does not replace the press trial – ideally it reduces it to one.
