Two groups of forming software

Before we look at individual programs, I will give you two drawers to sort them into.
The first drawer is dedicated, process-oriented software. It has templates for presses, tools, draw beads, rolling passes. It is meant for the process engineer, not for the FEM specialist. For sheet metal it is Ansys Forming, AutoForm or PAM-STAMP; for bulk forming Simufact, DEFORM, QForm or FORGE; for plastics Moldflow or Moldex3D.
The second drawer is general-purpose nonlinear FEM. There the user builds everything – parts, contacts, analysis steps. You get maximum flexibility and the possibility to write your own material subroutines. Typical examples are Abaqus, LS-DYNA, Ansys Mechanical and MSC Marc. They are used in research, in teaching and for unusual processes.
The table at the bottom sorts them by solver type – and that is a typical exam question. An implicit solver seeks equilibrium in every increment and can take large steps; this is how DEFORM and Abaqus Standard work, and it is the right choice for springback. An explicit solver takes very small stable time steps and handles contact very robustly; examples are LS-DYNA and Abaqus Explicit, and the typical application is sheet metal stamping. And a finite volume solver uses a fixed Eulerian mesh through which the material flows – the FV solver in Simufact and the flow solvers in Moldflow.
