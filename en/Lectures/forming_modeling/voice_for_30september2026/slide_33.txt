Key inputs: flow stress, friction, heat transfer

Three inputs decide most of the answer.
The first is the flow stress as a function of effective strain, strain rate and temperature. You take it from the material library – steels, aluminium, titanium, superalloys, copper – or from your own compression tests. Newer versions can even predict the flow stress of steels from their chemical composition using a neural network. We cover material models in detail next week.
The second is friction. In the slab method we used Coulomb friction – shear stress equals mu times pressure. In bulk forming DEFORM mostly uses the shear model: the friction stress is m times k, where k is the shear yield stress, that is, the flow stress over the square root of three. The friction factor m goes from zero – no friction – to one – full sticking.
The table gives guide values: about 0.08 to 0.12 for cold forming with good lubrication, about 0.2 for warm forming, 0.3 for lubricated hot forging and 0.7 to 1.0 for dry hot forging.
The third is heat transfer between the die and the workpiece. The heat transfer coefficient can be determined from thermocouple measurements using the Inverse HTC module.
And the most important point: flow stress and friction are the most sensitive inputs. If your result looks strange, check these two first.
