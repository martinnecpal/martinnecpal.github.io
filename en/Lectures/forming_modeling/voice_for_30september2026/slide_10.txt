Solving analytical models: MATLAB · Python · GAMS

Analytical does not mean "without software". Most analytical models today are solved with a script.
The table shows how forming problems map onto types of solvers. A closed-form formula, such as the drawing force, is simply evaluated – NumPy or MATLAB. The neutral point in rolling is a nonlinear equation – fzero in MATLAB, brentq in Python. Forging by the slab method or von Kármán's rolling equation is an ordinary differential equation – ode45 or solve_ivp. Derivations can be done symbolically in SymPy. The upper bound with an optimum die angle is a nonlinear optimisation problem – fmincon or GAMS with the CONOPT solver. And if you plan pass schedules where the number of passes must be an integer, you have a mixed-integer problem – that is where GAMS with BARON or Pyomo are strong. There are also complete open frameworks, for example PyRolL from TU Freiberg for groove rolling.

You can see how the force grows as the height decreases. That is the force–stroke curve – exactly the curve DEFORM will give you in the lab.
