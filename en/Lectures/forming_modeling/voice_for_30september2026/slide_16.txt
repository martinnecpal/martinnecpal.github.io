Ansys Forming — workflow and typical results

The interface follows the tabs you see on the left.
Process: CAD import, tipping – that is, rotating the part from its position in the vehicle to its position in the press – and the choice of accuracy. Blank: blank outline, material, forming limit curve, possibly tailor-welded blanks. Operations: drawing, trimming, flanging, draw beads. Then the Advance tab, which is the most interesting: springback compensation, trim line development and robustness analysis. Springback compensation means that the software iteratively modifies the tool surface until the part is within tolerance. That directly saves tool modifications. And finally Analysis with an automatic PowerPoint report.
On the right are typical results. The forming limit diagram and the formability index tell you whether the part will crack or wrinkle. Thinning shows local loss of thickness. Springback shows the shape after unloading. Stoning and zebra lines reveal visible surface defects – very important for outer body panels. Draw-in and skid marks describe the movement of the blank. And the press force determines the press size.
Two practical rules: have at least three elements on a 90-degree radius, and choose one of three presets – Fast to check the setup, Accurate as the standard, Very Accurate for springback compensation. Note also that in an explicit calculation the tool speeds are artificially increased to shorten the computing time.
