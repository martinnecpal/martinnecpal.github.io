The last elastic topic is hyperelasticity – materials like rubber that stretch several hundred percent and still recover. Linear elasticity fails here: look at the chart. Up to a few percent the two curves agree; at larger stretches the neo-Hookean curve departs from the straight line, and in compression it stiffens strongly. Instead of E and nu, a hyperelastic model uses a strain-energy function. Neo-Hookean has one constant, Mooney–Rivlin two. In forming you meet them in rubber-pad forming of aircraft sheet parts, in flexible dies, and when seals are part of an assembly. One rule: fit the constants from at least two deformation modes, because a fit to tension alone can be unstable in compression. That closes the elastic block.
