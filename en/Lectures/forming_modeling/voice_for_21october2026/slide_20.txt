For hot forming the standard approach combines temperature and strain rate into one parameter – the Zener–Hollomon parameter Z, strain rate times exp of Q over RT. Q is the activation energy for hot deformation: about 300 to 320 kilojoules per mole for carbon–manganese steels, half that for aluminium. The hyperbolic-sine law of Sellars and Tegart relates Z to the flow stress. It behaves like a power law at low stresses and like an exponential at high stresses, which fits hot-working data over many decades. Invert it, and you have stress as a function of Z – the chart. At 1000 degrees and one per second the steady-state stress is about 110 megapascals; going to 900 degrees and ten per second nearly doubles it. Norton–Hoff is the viscoplastic power law used in several European codes – stress proportional to strain rate to the m. In DEFORM's dropdown you find both the sinh form and the power-exponential form with the activation energy written as delta H.
