This deep dive is about the most widely used idea in hot forming: the Zener–Hollomon parameter, and the hyperbolic-sine law built on it. In nineteen forty-four Clarence Zener and John Hollomon published a study on the effect of strain rate on the plastic flow of steel. Their key observation: raising the strain rate has the same effect on the flow stress as lowering the temperature. So both can be combined into one variable – the temperature-compensated strain rate Z: strain rate times exp of Q over RT. Z has the unit of a strain rate, and the temperature must be absolute. Look at the numbers for a carbon–manganese steel. At 1000 degrees and one per second, Z is about 6.3 times ten to the twelfth. At 900 degrees and ten per second it is more than a hundred times higher – 7.8 times ten to the fourteenth; at 1200 degrees and 0.01 per second about ten thousand times lower. The chart shows lines of constant Z in the temperature–strain-rate plane. Every point on one line gives the same flow stress and a similar recrystallised grain size. That is very practical: a slow laboratory test at a higher temperature can represent a fast industrial press at a lower temperature.
