The mathematics in three lines. First, the inversion: arsinh written with a logarithm gives the flow stress directly as a function of Z. This is the formula FEM codes evaluate at every integration point. Second, differentiate with respect to the logarithm of strain rate and you get the strain-rate sensitivity m: tanh of alpha sigma divided by n alpha sigma. At low stress it tends to one over n – the creep value, around 0.2. At high stress it falls towards zero. The chart shows this for three values of n. Third, the temperature sensitivity follows from the same derivative multiplied by Q over R T squared. For our steel at 1000 degrees and one per second, alpha sigma is 1.32, m about 0.15, and cooling by only ten kelvin raises the flow stress by about three percent. That is why die chilling of the surface matters so much in hot forging.
