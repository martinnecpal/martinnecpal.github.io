Advanced high-strength steels are the toughest springback case, and the model most used for them is Yoshida–Uemori. It uses two surfaces: a small yield surface moving inside a larger bounding surface. That captures details plain kinematic hardening misses – the smooth transient after reversal, permanent softening and hardening stagnation. Another effect matters as much: the unloading modulus of steel drops by 10 to 20 percent after plastic straining. The formula here describes that degradation, and many springback studies show it is as important as the hardening model. Use the box as your springback checklist: elasto-plastic object, degraded modulus, kinematic or Yoshida–Uemori hardening, anisotropic yield and enough elements through the thickness. Yoshida–Uemori needs about seven constants from cyclic tests – not something you find in a generic library.
