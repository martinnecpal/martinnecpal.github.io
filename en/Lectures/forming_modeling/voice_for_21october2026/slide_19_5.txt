Two views of the sensitivities. Left, iron at 20 percent strain: the grey area is athermal – strain hardening and grain size – and it does not change with temperature. The red area is the thermal part; at room temperature and high rate it is about two-thirds of the total, and it almost disappears above 800 kelvin. Right, the strain-rate effect at 20 percent strain. From 10 to the minus 3 to 10 to the 4 per second, iron gains about 350 megapascals at room temperature, copper only about 100. The derivative at the bottom explains the pattern: rate sensitivity is C4 times T times the thermal stress. At very low temperature T is small; at high temperature the thermal stress is small; in between, the rate sensitivity is largest. Johnson–Cook, with its constant C, cannot show this – which is exactly why ZA describes BCC metals better at high rates.
