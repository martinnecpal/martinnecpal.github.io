Two relatives you will meet in other software. Norton–Hoff is a viscoplastic power law – stress proportional to strain rate to the m, with a consistency K that depends on temperature and strain. It is the basis of FORGE. Hensel–Spittel is a long empirical product of temperature, strain and strain-rate terms – up to nine exponents – used in the libraries of FORGE, QForm and Simufact. It has no Z, but it is fitted to the same hot compression data. In DEFORM the flow-stress list contains the sinh law and the power law exactly as you see on the right, with the activation energy written as delta H and the temperature absolute. Check which units DEFORM expects for delta H and R in your unit system before typing published values. A practical alternative many engineers prefer: evaluate the law in a spreadsheet or Python, and enter the result as a table of flow stress versus strain, strain rate and temperature. You can see the numbers, add strain hardening at small strains, and plot the curves in the material window before running.
