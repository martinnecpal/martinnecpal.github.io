The two classical isotropic criteria. Tresca says yielding starts when the maximum shear stress reaches a critical value – the hexagon. Von Mises uses the effective stress from the previous slide – the ellipse. They agree in uniaxial tension and in equibiaxial tension, and differ most in pure shear, by about 15 percent. Experiments on most metals lie closer to von Mises, and because the ellipse is smooth it is much easier for a finite element solver. That is why von Mises is the default in DEFORM and in almost every forming code. On the right, the DEFORM dropdown for the yield function: von Mises, the Hill quadratic forms, and further options. Bulk forming of isotropic billets – stay with von Mises. Sheets with rolling texture – the next slide.
