A constant c and n describe only one temperature. DEFORM allows each constant – c, n, m and y – to be a table versus temperature, which stretches the power law into warm forming. The trends are always the same: with rising temperature the material becomes softer, so c falls; recovery limits hardening, so n falls; and the strain-rate sensitivity m rises. The table illustrates this for a medium-carbon steel – the numbers show the trend, not data for a specific grade. Note the row at 200 degrees: dynamic strain ageing, m close to zero or negative – avoid that range. Two practical remarks. DEFORM interpolates linearly between the temperature points, so put enough points where properties change quickly. And in fast cold forging the billet heats by one to three hundred degrees from plastic work. So even a 'cold' simulation should be coupled with heat transfer and use c as a function of temperature. Above about 0.6 of the melting point, switch to the sinh law or a table.
