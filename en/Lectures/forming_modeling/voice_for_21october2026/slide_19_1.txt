This short sub-presentation goes deeper into one model, the Zerilli–Armstrong model, which I showed you on one slide a moment ago. We go down, slide by slide, and then come back up to the main line of the lecture. The model was published in nineteen eighty-seven by Frank Zerilli and Ronald Armstrong, who worked for the US Naval Surface Warfare Center. Their customers were people simulating impact, armour penetration and explosive loading – very high strain rates, a wide range of temperatures. The Johnson–Cook model had appeared four years earlier and was the standard. But Johnson–Cook is a fit: three independent brackets, the same shape for every metal. Zerilli and Armstrong noticed something experiments had shown for a long time: iron and copper react differently. In iron the yield stress itself depends strongly on temperature and strain rate. In copper the yield stress hardly changes – it is the hardening that changes. So instead of fitting, they derived the equation from how dislocations move, and let the crystal lattice choose the form. The table summarises the difference. Keep in mind the two key words: coupling of temperature and strain rate, and lattice-dependent form.
