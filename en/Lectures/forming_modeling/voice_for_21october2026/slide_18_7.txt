Johnson and Cook added a fracture model in nineteen eighty-five with the same structure. The strain to fracture depends on stress triaxiality – mean stress over effective stress – times a rate bracket and a temperature bracket. Damage accumulates as the sum of strain increments divided by the current failure strain; when it reaches one, the element fails and is usually deleted. The chart, for forty-three forty steel with the nineteen eighty-five constants: the failure strain falls steeply as triaxiality rises – from pure shear at zero, through uniaxial tension at one third, to notched bars with high triaxiality. Higher temperature raises the failure strain. The constants come from notched round bars of different notch radii, torsion tests, Hopkinson-bar and hot tests. The model is used for chip separation in machining, blanking and ballistic perforation. Compare it with the normalised Cockcroft–Latham criterion from the main lecture: Cockcroft–Latham has one constant; Johnson–Cook has five, but captures rate and temperature.
