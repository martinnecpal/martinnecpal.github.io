Now the equations. The first one in DEFORM's list is the power law: c times strain to the n, times strain rate to the m, plus y. Without the rate term and offset it is Hollomon's law; Swift adds a pre-strain epsilon zero. The exponent n is the strain-hardening exponent. In a tensile test it equals the uniform strain at necking – that is a nice check of your fit. Look at the chart: with n of 0.35 the curve keeps rising; with 0.1 it is almost flat after the first few percent. Stainless steel has n around 0.45, which is why it work-hardens so much in deep drawing. One practical point. Pure Hollomon gives zero stress at zero strain – an infinitely soft material at the start. DEFORM solves that with the y term, or you use Swift. In DEFORM c, n, m and y can each be a function of temperature, which is enough for simple warm forming.
