How are the four constants found? Start with hot compression tests, typically on a Gleeble: four to six temperatures and four strain rates. From each curve take the peak stress, or the steady-state stress. Step two: plot the logarithm of strain rate against the logarithm of stress – the slope gives n prime – and against stress itself – the slope gives beta. Alpha is beta over n prime. Step three: take the logarithm of the sinh law. Plotting ln sinh of alpha sigma against ln strain rate gives straight, parallel lines, one per temperature – the chart. The slope is one over n. Step four: the vertical spacing of those lines comes from temperature. Plot ln sinh against one over T at constant strain rate; the slope times n times R is the activation energy Q. Step five: the intercept gives A. Under the boxes, a published example for 30CrMoA steel: alpha 0.011, n 4.4, Q 262 kilojoules per mole, with a correlation of 0.98. This is the standard recipe – you will find it in hundreds of papers.
