The R-value, or Lankford coefficient, is measured in a tensile test of sheet: the ratio of width strain to thickness strain. R greater than one means the sheet resists thinning – good for deep drawing. Deep-drawing steels like DC04 have average R values of about 1.6 to 1.9. The variation of R with direction, delta r, produces ears on drawn cups – the sketch in the middle: four ears at 0 and 90 degrees when delta r is positive. On the right, the Hill yield locus for R of 1.8 and 0.6 compared with von Mises. High R stretches the locus along the biaxial direction – the sheet is stronger in equibiaxial stretching. Aluminium alloys, with R below one, show behaviour Hill 48 cannot describe well; Barlat's nineteen ninety-one non-quadratic criterion with exponent 8 for FCC metals was designed for that.
