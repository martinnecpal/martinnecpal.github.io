Two curves from the course textbook: at fixed strain rate, raising the temperature lowers the curve; at fixed temperature, raising the strain rate raises it. How strong each effect is depends on the homologous temperature – temperature divided by the melting point in kelvin. Cold forming, below about 0.3 of the melting point: strain hardening dominates and the rate sensitivity m is close to zero. Hot forming, above about half the melting point: hardening is balanced by recovery and dynamic recrystallisation, the curve flattens and the rate sensitivity rises to 0.1–0.2. Superplastic alloys go beyond 0.3. Do not forget that temperature itself changes during forming: about 90 percent of the plastic work becomes heat. In fast cold forging the billet can heat up by a hundred degrees or more, so even a 'cold' simulation needs thermal data.
