Zerilli and Armstrong derived their model from dislocation mechanics. The key physical insight is the difference between crystal lattices. In body-centred cubic metals – ferritic steels – temperature and strain rate act mainly on the yield stress, because dislocations must overcome the lattice resistance. In face-centred cubic metals – copper, aluminium, nickel – they act on the hardening term. The chart uses the original nineteen eighty-seven constants. Between liquid-nitrogen temperature and room temperature iron loses about half of its strength, copper only about a third – and copper's initial yield stress hardly changes at all. That is the well-known low-temperature brittleness of ferritic steels versus the toughness of copper and austenitic steels. DEFORM offers the BCC form in its list. Use ZA rather than Johnson–Cook when you need a physically based temperature–rate coupling, for example for high-rate forming over a wide temperature range.
