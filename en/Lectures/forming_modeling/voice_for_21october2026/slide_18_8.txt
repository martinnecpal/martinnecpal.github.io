Where is Johnson–Cook used? First and foremost in machining – it is the default flow-stress model in most cutting simulations, including DEFORM-3D Machining. The sketch shows why: in the primary shear zone strains reach one to five at strain rates up to a million per second, and at the tool–chip interface the temperature rises to 800 or 1000 degrees. Combined with the Johnson–Cook damage model it reproduces chip segmentation. Then its original field: impact, ballistics and Taylor tests, where it is robust and cheap in explicit codes. Crash simulation of car body structures, high-speed forming – electromagnetic, explosive and hammer forging – and blanking and punching. And one line where it is not recommended: hot forging above about 0.6 of the melting point. There the sinh law or measured tables are the right tools – recrystallisation is outside Johnson–Cook's world.
