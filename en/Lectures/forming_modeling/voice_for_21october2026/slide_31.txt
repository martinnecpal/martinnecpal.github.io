The DEFORM creep options. The most common is Norton's power law: creep rate proportional to stress to the n. It describes steady-state creep and is used for stress relaxation. Bailey–Norton adds time to a power below one, which also captures primary creep. Perzyna is a viscoplastic overstress model: creep rate depends on how far the stress exceeds the yield stress. Soderberg and the exponential law cover low and high stress ranges, and the tabular option takes measured data directly. A note on the Norton exponent: three to eight means dislocation creep; about one means diffusion creep, typical of fine-grained or superplastic materials. And creep data extrapolate even worse than flow stress – fit them to the stress and temperature range you simulate.
