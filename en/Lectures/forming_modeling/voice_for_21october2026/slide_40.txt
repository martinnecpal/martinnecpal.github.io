Two more tabs. Diffusion is used for carburising and nitriding: Fick's second law with a temperature-dependent diffusion coefficient. For carbon in austenite, D zero is about 2.3 times ten to the minus five square metres per second and the activation energy about 148 kilojoules per mole. The carbon content then changes the Ms temperature, the hardness and the TTT curves – so diffusion couples to the phase data. The Electro-magnetic tab holds electrical resistivity and magnetic permeability for induction heating. The key physics is the skin depth. Below the Curie point steel is ferromagnetic and the current flows in a thin skin; above about 770 degrees the permeability drops to one, the skin depth grows roughly tenfold and the heating zone moves inward. Without temperature-dependent permeability you cannot predict the hardened contour on this gear.
