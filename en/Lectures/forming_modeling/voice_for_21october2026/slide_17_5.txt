This is the most important slide of the sub-presentation. I generated tensile-test points between 2 and 20 percent strain and fitted all four laws to them by least squares. In the measured range – the blue band – you cannot tell them apart; the errors are below one percent. Now extrapolate to a strain of two, which is normal in cold heading. Voce gives about 667 megapascals, Hollomon 714, Swift 1184 and Ludwik 1401. That is a factor of two between the lowest and the highest – from the same data. Press force and die stresses scale with the flow stress, so the choice of the law alone changes the predicted load by roughly plus or minus thirty-five percent. Which one is right? None of the tensile data can tell you. Physically, Voce's saturation suits aluminium and copper; steels with little recovery keep hardening more like a power law. Hence the rule: fit to data that cover the strain range of the process. For cold forging that means compression or torsion tests to strains of one or two. Tensile-only fits are fine for sheet forming, not for bulk forming.
