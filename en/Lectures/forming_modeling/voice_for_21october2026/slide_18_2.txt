Let us look at each bracket on its own. The first is simply Ludwik's hardening law – the flow curve at the reference strain rate and temperature. The second, left chart, is the rate factor. It is linear in the logarithm of strain rate: with C of 0.0134, four decades of strain rate give twelve percent more stress. Two practical details. Below the reference rate the factor is less than one, so choose the reference as your lowest test rate, or let the code clip it. And because C is constant, the model cannot show the steep upturn of flow stress that many metals show above about a thousand to ten thousand per second. The third, right chart, is thermal softening: one minus the homologous temperature to the power m. With m below one the material softens early; with m above one it keeps its strength longer. At the melting point the stress is zero. Below the reference temperature T star becomes negative, and a non-integer power of a negative number is undefined – most codes simply set T star to zero there.
