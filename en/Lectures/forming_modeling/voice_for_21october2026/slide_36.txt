Emissivity controls radiation from free surfaces. Radiation goes with the fourth power of absolute temperature, so for a billet at 1200 degrees it dominates the heat loss during transfer from the furnace to the press. Oxidised steel has an emissivity of 0.7 to 0.9 – the 0.7 you saw in the screenshot. Polished aluminium is below 0.1 – which is why aluminium billets cool slowly by radiation. The picture is open-die cogging of a large ingot: the process takes hours, and most of the temperature loss happens by radiation between the passes. Here emissivity is a first-order input. Density has three roles: mass and inertia for hammers and screw presses, the specific-heat option, and density as a function of temperature, which produces volume change in heat treatment.
