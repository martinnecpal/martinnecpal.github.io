Where is the power law the right choice? Wherever strain hardening dominates and temperature and rate effects are small: cold forging, cold heading and cold extrusion – as long as you have data to large strains. Wire and bar drawing, where strain accumulates over many passes. Sheet stamping and deep drawing, where Swift or Hollomon together with the r-value is the standard description. Bending and roll forming. And with temperature tables, warm forging. For sheet metal n has another very practical meaning. The empirical Keeler–Brazier relation for low-carbon steels links the lowest point of the forming limit diagram – the plane-strain limit – to n and the sheet thickness. The chart shows it: a sheet with n of 0.2 and one millimetre thickness can take about 35 percent major strain in plane strain; with n of 0.1 only half of that. That is why press shops ask for the n-value on every coil certificate.
