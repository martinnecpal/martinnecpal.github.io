The last thermal item is not in the Material window, but it belongs here: heat transfer between die and workpiece. The heat flux is h times the temperature difference, and h depends on contact pressure, time, lubricant and scale. It is set in the Inter-object dialog. DEFORM's default for forming contact is about 11 kilowatts per square metre-kelvin, and 0.02 for convection at free surfaces. Where do better values come from? The three pictures are SFTC's Inverse HTC module: you run a test with thermocouples in the die or the part, then DEFORM changes h until the simulated temperature curves match the measured ones, and returns the fitted h. Keep this idea of inverse analysis – it is the same trick used to correct friction in compression tests for flow stress. Simulation is not only a user of material data; it is also a tool to measure them.
