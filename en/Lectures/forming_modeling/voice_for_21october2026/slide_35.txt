Here is the confusing one. DEFORM's default is heat capacity – energy per volume per degree – not specific heat per mass. For steel: 7850 kilograms per cubic metre times 440 joules per kilogram-kelvin gives about 3.45 megajoules per cubic metre per kelvin, which in DEFORM units is 3.45 newtons per square millimetre per degree. If you type 440 there, your billet will practically never heat up. The alternative is the Specific heat option; then DEFORM multiplies by density itself – which is the reason density is in the Thermal tab. The chart is the Eurocode specific heat of carbon steel. Notice the peak at about 735 degrees – the alpha to gamma transformation absorbs latent heat. In a Regular material this peak represents the latent heat. In a Mixture the latent heat is entered in the transformation data – so you must not have it twice.
