Let me start with a small numerical experiment. We upset a cylinder 20 millimetres in diameter and 30 high down to 15. Same geometry, same friction coefficient. The only difference is the flow curve. Curve A is a typical library curve for a C45 steel in cold condition; curve B is what you might measure on your own annealed bar of the same nominal grade. The forces at the end of the stroke are about 540 and 427 kilonewtons – a difference of roughly 27 percent. If you refine the mesh you change the force by a few percent. If you choose the wrong flow curve you change it by tens of percent – and you might order the wrong press. This is the meaning of 'garbage in, garbage out'. The solver is very good at solving the equations; it has no idea whether the material you gave it is the material on the shop floor. That is your job, and it is the job of today's lecture. Look at the box at the bottom: force and energy, temperature, die fill, damage, springback, microstructure – every one of these outputs depends on a different part of the material data. We will meet all of them.
