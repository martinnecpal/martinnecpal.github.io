The Thermal tab has four lines: conductivity, heat capacity – or specific heat, there is a switch – emissivity and density. They enter the heat equation on the slide: heat stored equals heat conducted plus heat generated by plastic work, by phase transformation and by induction. Note the beta times sigma times epsilon-dot term: that is the plastic work converted to heat, with beta around 0.9 – the coupling between mechanics and temperature. The units again: conductivity in newtons per second per degree, which is the same as watts per metre-kelvin, so you can copy handbook values directly. Heat capacity is per volume, in newtons per square millimetre per degree. That one confuses people – two slides from now.
