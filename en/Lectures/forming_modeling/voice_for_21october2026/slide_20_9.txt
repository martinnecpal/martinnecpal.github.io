Where is all this used? Practically everywhere above half the melting point. In hot closed-die forging the sinh law gives the flow stress for die fill and press load, and Z feeds the grain-size prediction. In hot rolling it gives the mean flow stress of each pass, and Z decides how much the material recrystallises between passes. Aluminium extrusion is the classic application: the sinh model of Sheppard and Wright has been the standard for decades, used to predict exit temperature and the maximum extrusion speed before surface cracking. For isothermal forging of titanium and nickel alloys the strain rates are low, close to the creep regime, and the strain-rate sensitivity from the sinh law matters. And for process design, the equal-Z idea lets us transfer laboratory results to the press, and processing maps help choose a safe window.
