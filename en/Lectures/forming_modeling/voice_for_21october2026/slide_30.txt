Creep is slow plastic flow at constant stress. It becomes important above roughly 0.4 of the melting temperature – for steel that is from about 450 degrees, for aluminium near room temperature for long times, for lead even at room temperature. The creep curve has three stages: primary creep, where the rate decreases as the material hardens; secondary or steady-state creep at a minimum rate, where hardening and recovery balance; and tertiary creep, where voids and necking accelerate it to rupture. Higher stress – the red curve – shortens everything. In DEFORM creep is an additional strain-rate term next to elastic, plastic and thermal strain, switched on in the Plastic tab for elasto-plastic objects – the dialog on the right.
