No model is universal. ZA is physically based only below roughly half the melting temperature: there is no dynamic recovery, no recrystallisation, no phase change. In the BCC form the strain hardening is athermal, so at high temperature it over-predicts hardening. And above about ten thousand per second a different mechanism – viscous dislocation drag – takes over. An independent comparison by Banerjee for copper found ZA very good for room-temperature Taylor tests – the simplest accurate option – but it over-estimates the temperature dependence at high rates and gives larger errors near 1200 kelvin. For DEFORM: choose the ZA entry in the flow-stress list and enter the constants. Published constants assume temperature in kelvin and stress in megapascals – check your problem's unit system before you type them in. Run a coupled thermal simulation, because adiabatic heating is a large part of the physics at high rates. Plot the curves in the material window and compare them with the test data. And for hot forming above about 0.6 of the melting point, use tabular data or sinh-Arrhenius instead.
