Last topic: fracture. DEFORM offers many ductile damage criteria; the most used is normalised Cockcroft–Latham. Damage is the integral of the maximum principal stress over effective stress along the strain path, counting only tensile stress. When D reaches the critical value C, a crack is expected. In upsetting – the sketch – the bulging surface has tensile hoop stress, so damage grows there first; that is where cold-heading cracks appear. In cross-wedge rolling, on the right, the centre of the bar is loaded in alternating tension and can open – the Mannesmann effect. The important point for material data: C is not universal. You calibrate it by simulating a test – upsetting, tension, notched bars – to the strain at which the real specimen cracked. Its value depends on the steel cleanliness and temperature. A C value from a different heat of material is only a rough guide.
