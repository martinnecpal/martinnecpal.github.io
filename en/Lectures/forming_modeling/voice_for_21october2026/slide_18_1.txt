The Johnson–Cook model was published in nineteen eighty-three by Gordon Johnson and William Cook of Honeywell, at the International Symposium on Ballistics in The Hague. Their aim was practical: a model simple enough to run in explicit impact codes – their own EPIC code – and with constants that can be obtained from a few standard tests. They used torsion tests over a range of strain rates, static tension, and Hopkinson-bar tension tests at elevated temperatures. They then validated the model on Taylor cylinder impact tests with strains above two – tests that were not used in the fitting. Two years later they published the companion fracture model. On the right: the equation and the five constants. A, B and n come from quasi-static tests; C from high-rate tests; m from tests at elevated temperature. Plus three reference values: the reference strain rate, often one per second; the reference temperature; and the melting temperature.
