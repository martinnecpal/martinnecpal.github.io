Here is the physics. A moving dislocation meets two kinds of obstacles. Long-range obstacles – the stress fields of other dislocations, grain boundaries – are wide; thermal vibration of atoms cannot help to pass them. They give the athermal part, sigma G. Short-range obstacles are narrow peaks: in BCC metals the Peierls hills of the lattice itself, in FCC metals the forest dislocations that a moving dislocation must cut through. Here thermal vibration helps. The applied stress lifts the dislocation part of the way, a thermal fluctuation does the rest. The rate of successful jumps follows an Arrhenius law. From it come the two familiar effects. If the temperature rises, thermal fluctuations are stronger, so less stress is needed. If the strain rate rises, the dislocation has less time to wait for a lucky fluctuation, so more stress is needed. And the grain size adds the Hall–Petch term. So the total flow stress is athermal plus thermal plus Hall–Petch. The whole ZA model is about writing the thermal part correctly.
