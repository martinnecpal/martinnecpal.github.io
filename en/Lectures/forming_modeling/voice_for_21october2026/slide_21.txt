The dropdown list on the left shows that DEFORM offers more than the classical equations. Let me go through the table. A dislocation-density model computes flow stress from the dislocation density, which itself evolves with strain, strain rate and temperature – useful when the material history matters, for example between hits in multi-step hot forging. Texture and crystal plasticity models start from grain orientations; they matter for titanium, magnesium and sheet earing. Since version 13 there is a deep-neural-network predictor that estimates a flow curve from chemical composition – very handy in early design when you have no test data, but treat it as an estimate. And if nothing fits, you can write your own subroutine and compile it into the solver. Finally, the Material Suite, a separate SFTC tool, fits flow stress from compression tests and computes TTT diagrams and JMAK constants.
