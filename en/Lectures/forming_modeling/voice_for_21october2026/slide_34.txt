Conductivity decides how fast heat moves inside the part. The chart: carbon steel starts around 54 watts per metre-kelvin and falls to about 27 at 800 degrees; austenitic stainless starts at 15 and rises. Using a room-temperature constant for a hot forging is therefore a factor-of-two error. The table shows the range: copper conducts 50 times better than titanium. Titanium and nickel alloys conduct heat poorly, so the surface touching a cold die chills while the core stays hot – and in fast deformation the heat stays in shear bands. That is why titanium forgings are so sensitive to die temperature and why titanium is hard to machine.
