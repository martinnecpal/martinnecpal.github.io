The most common form of flow stress in DEFORM is a table: a set of curves, each for one temperature and one strain rate, each sampled at several strains. On the left you see the DEFORM graph and the table behind it. Between the table points the program has to interpolate. Strain rate in forging spans five or six decades, so it matters how. On the right I took four points of that table – strain rates 1, 10, 100 and 1000 – and asked for the value at 50 per second. Linear interpolation gives about 1012 megapascals, logarithmic about 1028. The difference here is modest, but it grows when the table is coarse. Flow stress is roughly linear in the logarithm of strain rate, so logarithmic interpolation is the physically sensible choice. A good table has five to eight temperatures and four to six strain rates covering the process. That last condition is the subject of the next slide.
