Here the difference becomes visible. Both charts are computed from the nineteen eighty-seven constants at a strain rate of one thousand per second, for 77, 296 and 600 kelvin. Left, Armco iron, BCC. The three curves are parallel: temperature shifts the whole curve up or down by the same amount, because it acts on the yield stress. At liquid-nitrogen temperature iron is more than twice as strong at yield as at room temperature. Right, OFHC copper, FCC. All curves start at nearly the same yield stress and then fan out: at low temperature copper hardens faster. Temperature acts on the hardening slope, not on the yield point. Practical consequences: ferritic steels become brittle at low temperatures and are strongly rate-sensitive; austenitic steels, copper and aluminium are not. Hexagonal metals like titanium and magnesium show both features, so for them a combined form with both exponential terms was proposed later.
