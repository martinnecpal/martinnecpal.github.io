Elastic data are often left at library defaults, so let us be clear about when they matter. For a rigid-plastic billet – the standard object type for forging – DEFORM neglects elastic strain. Young's modulus is simply not used. The plastic strains are hundreds of percent, the elastic ones a fraction of a percent. Elastic data become essential in three cases. First, elastic dies: the die stress picture here, die inserts and shrink rings. Second, elasto-plastic workpieces where we care about what happens at unloading – springback in sheet forming, residual stresses. Third, heat treatment, where the total strain is the sum of elastic, plastic, thermal and transformation parts, and distortion comes from their interplay.
