What happens outside the table? The program has to guess, either by holding the last value or by extending the last slope. Left chart: the material follows Johnson–Cook, but the table only goes to 10 per second. Used in a machining simulation at ten thousand per second, the clamped table underestimates the flow stress by about 8.2 percent – and that is at room temperature, with a mild rate effect. The right chart is a map you should keep in your head. Horizontal axis: strain rate on a log scale. Vertical: homologous temperature. Dashed boxes are where tests can reach; solid boxes are where processes run. Hot forging overlaps well with Gleeble compression. Machining sits at strain rates only the Hopkinson bar approaches. Service creep is at strain rates a million times slower than any forming test. So the practical rule: after the first run, open the post-processor, check the maximum strain, strain rate and temperature, and compare them with the table. DEFORM will not warn you.
