Z is not only about stress. It controls the microstructure. The dynamically recrystallised grain size follows a power law in Z with a small negative exponent, typically 0.1 to 0.3. High temperature and slow forming – low Z – give coarse grains; low temperature and fast forming – high Z – give fine grains. That is why the last forging step is often done colder and faster. These equations are the ones in DEFORM's grain data, which we meet again in block 8. Z also underlies processing maps. In Prasad's dynamic materials model the strain-rate sensitivity m – which we just derived from the sinh law – is turned into an efficiency of power dissipation, two m over m plus one. Regions with high efficiency, around 30 to 55 percent, usually correspond to dynamic recrystallisation – a safe window. Where the instability parameter becomes negative, the material tends to form shear bands or cracks. Process engineers use these maps to choose forging temperature and speed.
