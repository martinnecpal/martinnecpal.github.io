How do you get K and n? Take the logarithm of Hollomon's law: ln sigma equals ln K plus n ln epsilon – a straight line on a log–log plot. The procedure: convert the tensile test to true stress and true plastic strain – subtract the elastic part. Use only the uniform range, from about two percent strain to the maximum force; ISO one oh two seven five standardises this as the n-value of sheet. Fit a straight line: the slope is n, the stress at strain one is K. On the chart, synthetic points with n = 0.24 give back n = 0.237. For DEFORM's form with the y term, first fix y as the yield stress, then fit the logarithm of sigma minus y. And the important practical point: a tensile test ends at necking, around 20 to 30 percent strain. Cold forging reaches strains of two or three. For bulk forming you need compression tests to a strain of about one, corrected for friction. If the log–log plot is not straight, the material simply does not follow a power law – use Voce or a table.
