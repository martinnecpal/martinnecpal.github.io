Hardening describes how the yield surface changes as plastic strain accumulates. On the left you see the effect: after deformation the material needs a higher stress to deform further. In isotropic hardening the yield surface keeps its centre and grows uniformly – the left circle. The radius is simply the flow stress at the current plastic strain. That is exactly what the flow curve describes, so isotropic hardening needs no extra data. It is the DEFORM default and it is exact as long as loading goes in one direction – which is true for most forging.
