A flow curve comes from a uniaxial test, but in a forging every point has a full three-dimensional stress state – nine components, six independent. How do we compare them? We condense the tensor into one number, the effective stress, and say the material yields when the effective stress reaches the flow stress. First we remove the hydrostatic part, the mean stress, because pressure alone does not make dense metals yield. What remains is the deviatoric stress. The von Mises effective stress is the square root of three halves of its double contraction – or, in principal stresses, the familiar formula with the three differences. How exactly the effective stress is computed is set by the yield function in the Plastic tab. That is the next three slides.
