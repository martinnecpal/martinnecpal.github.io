Johnson–Cook is the standard model for machining and impact. It is a product of three brackets: hardening, rate and thermal softening. Each bracket has its own constants and they do not interact – which makes fitting easy, and is also its weakness. The constants here are for AISI ten forty-five, published by Jaspers and Dautzenberg from Hopkinson bar tests, and the chart is computed from them. Going from 1 to ten thousand per second raises the curve by about 12 percent; heating to 700 degrees removes roughly half the strength. DEFORM's version in the dropdown list has two extra parameters – a power on the strain rate and a more flexible temperature bracket. The model is excellent for machining, where strain rates are extreme and temperatures moderate. It is poor near the melting point and in hot forging, where recrystallisation couples temperature and strain rate – which the separate brackets cannot represent.
