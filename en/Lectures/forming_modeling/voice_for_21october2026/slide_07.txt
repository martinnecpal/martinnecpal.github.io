Now the trap. The screenshot two slides ago shows copper C10100 with a Young's modulus of 16679. Sixteen thousand megapascals for copper? That would be seven times too soft. The number is in ksi – the material was saved in English units. 16679 ksi is 115 gigapascals, which is correct for copper. The same for thermal expansion per degree Fahrenheit, and the reference temperature of 68 Fahrenheit, which is 20 Celsius. DEFORM sets the unit system for the whole problem. If you bring an English-unit material into an SI problem, convert it with the Unit conversion button – do not retype numbers by hand. Note the SI units DEFORM uses: stress in megapascals, density in tonnes per cubic millimetre – 7.7 times ten to the minus nine for steel – and heat capacity per volume in newtons per square millimetre per degree. The right table answers the question 'which tabs do I really need?' An isothermal forging needs only the Plastic tab. As soon as temperature changes, Thermal data are needed. Elastic data only when elastic strains matter – springback, residual stress, die stress. Heat treatment needs everything.
