Why should hot deformation follow an Arrhenius law at all? Above about half the melting temperature, dislocations no longer just glide. They climb and cross-slip around obstacles, and climb needs vacancies to diffuse. Diffusion is a thermally activated process with its own Arrhenius term, so the strain rate inherits it. In that sense hot working is high-temperature creep, only a million times faster. At the same time dislocations multiply – hardening – and are removed by dynamic recovery and dynamic recrystallisation – softening. When the two balance, we reach a steady-state stress, and the steady-state stress is a function of Z alone. The table gives typical apparent activation energies. For pure aluminium it is close to the activation energy of self-diffusion, about 142 kilojoules per mole. Alloying, solutes and particles raise the apparent value: 260 to 320 for carbon and low-alloy steels, 400 to 460 for austenitic stainless steels. Titanium alloy Ti-6Al-4V shows about 246 in the beta field but more than 600 in the two-phase field, because the phase balance changes with temperature. A very high apparent Q is a warning that the microstructure itself is changing.
