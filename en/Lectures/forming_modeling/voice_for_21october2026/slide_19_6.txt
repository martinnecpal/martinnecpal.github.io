Here are real constants. For OFHC copper the original paper gives C2 of 890 megapascals, C3 of 0.0028 and C4 of 1.15 times ten to the minus four. C0 is about 65 megapascals, made of an athermal 46.5 plus the Hall–Petch term for a grain size of 73 micrometres. For Armco iron C1 is 1033, C3 about 0.007, C4 about 4 times ten to the minus four, and athermal hardening 266 times strain to the 0.29. Tantalum, used for explosively formed projectiles, has a similar BCC set. DEFORM offers the BCC form directly in the flow-stress dropdown – exactly the expression you see here. On the right, the modified ZA model by Samantaray, Mandal and Bhaduri from two thousand nine. It was developed for hot deformation, fitted to Gleeble compression data. It couples strain into the temperature term and temperature into the rate term. It fits hot-working data of steels well – correlation around 0.995 for a titanium-modified austenitic stainless steel – but note that it has lost the dislocation interpretation; it is a semi-empirical fit with a ZA-like structure. The model is available in DEFORM, LS-DYNA, Ansys explicit codes and via user subroutines in Abaqus.
