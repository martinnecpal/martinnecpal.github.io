Where do you meet creep in forming? First, stress relaxation. A shrink-fit ring around a hot die insert is assembled with a certain preload; at working temperature the preload drops by creep. The chart shows Norton relaxation: the stress falls fast at first and then slowly – after tens of hours a large part of the preload is gone. Second, isothermal and hot-die forging of titanium and nickel alloys at very low strain rates, and superplastic forming – there the forming process itself is creep-like. Third, stress-relief annealing and hot sizing, where we want creep to remove residual stress. The photo shows the classic dead-weight creep frame – a test that runs for thousands of hours. That is creep. Now thermal data – fifteen minutes.
