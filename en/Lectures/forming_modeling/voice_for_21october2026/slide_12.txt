Flow stress is the true stress needed to keep the material flowing plastically at a given strain, strain rate and temperature. This equation – sigma bar as a function of epsilon bar, epsilon bar dot and T – is the one line you should remember from today. Left: engineering and true curves. Tensile machines give force and elongation, which are engineering values. FEM needs true stress and logarithmic strain; the conversion formulas are here, and they are valid only until necking starts. In the middle: loading beyond the yield point leaves permanent strain. A rigid-plastic code like DEFORM uses only the plastic part of the curve – so the flow curve in DEFORM starts at the initial yield stress at zero plastic strain.
