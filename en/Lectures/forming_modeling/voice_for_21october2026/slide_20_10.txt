Finally, the limits. The sinh law describes a steady-state or peak stress; for the full curve you need strain compensation or a recrystallisation model. The constants are valid only in the tested window, and a phase change – ferrite to austenite, alpha to beta in titanium – changes the activation energy, so do not fit across it. It is not a model for cold or warm forming. And at high strain rates the test specimen heats up, so correct the data before fitting. Summary in four points: Z combines temperature and strain rate in one parameter, with absolute temperature. Its physics is thermally activated, diffusion-controlled flow. The sinh law joins the power and exponential laws, and inverts to a closed-form flow stress. And Z also sets the recrystallised grain size and the safe processing windows. Five questions to finish. For the first one, take out your calculators: about 1.3 times ten to the twelfth. Then we go back up to the main lecture.
