Both elastic properties depend on temperature. The left chart is the Eurocode reduction factor for the modulus of carbon steel: at 600 degrees only about a third of the room-temperature value remains, and close to forging temperature almost nothing. If you simulate quenching from 850 degrees with a constant modulus of 210 gigapascals, you will badly overestimate the stresses at the start of cooling. On the right, thermal expansion of several steels as stored in DEFORM. Notice the field 'Reference temperature' in the Elastic tab. DEFORM uses a secant coefficient: the thermal strain is alpha times the difference from the reference temperature. If you copy instantaneous values from a handbook, they are not the same thing – check which one your source gives. And for heat treatment: austenite expands more than ferrite, so in a mixture each phase needs its own alpha – the volume change at transformation is a separate input in the Transformation dialog.
