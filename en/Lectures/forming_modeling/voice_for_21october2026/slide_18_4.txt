The calibration follows the brackets, which is the big advantage of the model. Step one: A is the yield stress in a quasi-static test at room temperature. Step two: subtract A, take logarithms, and the slope of the log–log plot gives n and the intercept B. Step three, the chart: at a fixed strain, divide the stress at each strain rate by the stress at the reference rate and plot it against the logarithm of strain rate. The slope is C. With synthetic quasi-static and Hopkinson-bar points the regression returns 0.0137. Step four: tests at elevated temperature; the logarithm of one minus the stress ratio against the logarithm of T star gives m. Step five, the check: Hopkinson-bar specimens heat up adiabatically, so correct those curves first; then validate on a test you did not use for fitting. The alternative is a global least-squares fit of all five constants, or inverse FEM – smaller overall error, but you lose the transparency.
