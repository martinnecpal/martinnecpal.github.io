The power law is the simplest flow-stress model in DEFORM, so it is worth knowing where it comes from and what its relatives are. This short sub-presentation covers the family of hardening laws, the meaning of n and m, how to measure them, and the one big trap – extrapolation. Ludwik proposed in nineteen oh-nine an initial yield stress plus a power of strain. Hollomon in nineteen forty-five dropped the initial yield stress – the famous K epsilon to the n, which gives the n-value of sheet metal. Swift in nineteen fifty-two added a pre-strain epsilon zero, so the stress at zero strain is not zero. Voce in nineteen forty-eight took a different route: an exponential approach to a saturation stress. The first three grow without limit; Voce saturates. That difference will matter a lot on slide five. DEFORM's power law is Ludwik's form with an added strain-rate term: c times strain to the n times strain rate to the m, plus y. Remember that all of these are empirical fits to the shape of the curve – none of them is derived from dislocation theory.
