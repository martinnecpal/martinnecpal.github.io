The weaknesses come from the same structure that makes the model simple. Because the brackets are multiplied, the rate sensitivity is the same at every temperature, and the hardening is the same at every rate and temperature. The constant C cannot reproduce the steep rise of flow stress above about ten thousand per second. Accuracy falls at high temperatures, there is no recovery or recrystallisation, and hardening is unbounded. For BCC metals with a strongly temperature-dependent yield stress, Zerilli–Armstrong is often better. Hence the many modified versions. DEFORM's own form adds a power-law rate term and a more flexible thermal bracket. Lin and co-workers in twenty ten proposed a version with a quadratic hardening term and an exponential temperature term whose slope depends on strain rate – that introduces the missing coupling. Others add a quadratic term in the logarithm of strain rate to capture the high-rate upturn. Each modification adds constants; the original form remains the default in most codes.
