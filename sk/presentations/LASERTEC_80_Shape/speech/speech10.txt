SLIDE 10 — Applications: Fields of Application
===============================================

The LASERTEC 80 Shape covers a wide range of industrial applications. Let me go through them.

The primary application is injection mold texturing — engraving fine surface structures onto mold cavities. The texture transfers to every plastic part produced with that mold, so one laser-textured mold can create millions of parts with identical surface finish. This is far more cost-effective than chemical etching.

The second application is chip-breaker manufacturing on cutting inserts made from sintered carbide. In experiments at CE5AM, chip-breaker geometries were laser-machined directly into cemented carbide inserts — a material that is extremely hard and difficult to machine by conventional methods. The laser removes material without contact, making it ideal for such hard and brittle substrates.

Third, functional surface texturing for tribological purposes — creating patterns of micro-dimples, grooves, or hatching on surfaces to modify friction and wear behaviour. This was extensively studied at our faculty, including experiments on pressing tool faces to improve the deep drawing process.

Fourth, five-axis laser structuring of large forming tools — such as stamping dies — where the complex three-dimensional geometry requires simultaneous five-axis movement.

Fifth, machining of narrow molds with exhaust channels — geometries inaccessible to conventional cutting tools.

And sixth, selective coating ablation — removing PVD or CVD coatings from specific areas of a coated part with a track width down to 40 micrometres.

The machine is compatible with sintered carbide, titanium alloys, tool steels, and coated surfaces.
